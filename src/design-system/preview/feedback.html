What a screen shows when it is not showing data. Seven copies of the confirm dialog, five retry blocks across three different minimum heights, and three rival empty states are all one component each now.

Alerts

The status was not changedThe order service rejected the change. It is still processing.
Two documents are expiringThe trade licence and TIN certificate expire within 30 days.
Supplier approvedThey can now receive orders.
Read-onlyYour role can view suppliers but not edit them.

Errors say what happened and what is still true — "It is still processing" is the part that stops an operator chasing an order the supplier never dispatched. They never apologise and never show the server's own words: raw messages were being rendered straight onto an unauthenticated password-reset page.

Empty is not the same as broken

No orders yet
Orders placed by retailers will appear here.
No cancelled orders
Try another status filter.
Orders could not be loaded
The order service did not respond. Nothing has changed.
Fraud detection is not running
This is not a clean result — no checks have been performed.

Four distinct facts that a single "no data" state conflates. The last one is why EmptyState has variants at all: a fraud screen showing zero flags reads as "no fraud detected" when the truth is "detection is not running" — worse than an empty state, because it looks like a negative result rather than no result.

Loading

Every grey bar is aria-hidden — a rectangle is not information. But that left the region-level skeletons announcing nothing at all: a screen-reader user waiting on a slow table heard silence and could not tell it from an empty one. The composites carry role="status"; nest one inside another and you pass label={null}, because two live regions announce the same load twice.

Confirm

Move to Cancelled?

Cancelling releases the committed stock and notifies the retailer. This cannot be undone from here.

Cancel comes before confirm — two of the seven hand-rolled copies had it the other way, and the dialog exists precisely when the other option is expensive. A backdrop click does not dismiss: a confirmation must be answered, not lost to a stray click.

The bug in this exact markup. The backdrop carried the page-level overlay step (40) while the panel was position: relative at z-index: auto. Both sit inside one container at modal, and within a stacking context a positive z-index paints above auto — so the backdrop covered the panel and every button in every confirm dialog in the app was unclickable, Cancel included. The whole test suite passed: jsdom does no hit testing and fireEvent.click dispatches straight at the node. It took elementFromPoint in a real browser to see it.
