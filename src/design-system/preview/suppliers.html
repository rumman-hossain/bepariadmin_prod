Suppliers

The directory an operator opens to answer two questions: what is waiting on me, and where is the supplier who just phoned. The screen it replaces could answer neither.

1 · The list

There was no status filter at all. Status was a column, never a filter, so the one question this directory exists to answer — which suppliers are waiting for approval — could not be asked. The whole Review → Approve/Reject workflow was invisible from the screen that is meant to lead to it.

2 · The paperwork quartet

Four marks, always in the same order: trade licence, TIN, VAT, NID — the certificates a supplier is actually onboarded with. Filled means on file.

A percentage would say how many are missing. Which one is missing is what decides the next action: chasing a VAT certificate is a different call from chasing an owner's NID. The fixed order means position is identity — reading down the column, a gap in the third slot is the VAT column, every time — so the pattern is scannable without a legend, and the count beside it names the gap for anyone who is not scanning.

An empty result is not the same event as an empty directory, and it must not borrow its words. "No suppliers yet" over a filtered view tells an operator the business has no suppliers.

What this screen deliberately does not have

No five equal count tiles. Only one of the five is a queue. Review is work with a person's name on it; Active, Suspended, Rejected and Removed are facts about the world. Giving them equal weight would tell an operator to treat them the same, and the number they came for is the one that means somebody is waiting.

No completeness percentage. "75% complete" is a number nobody can act on. Four marks in a fixed order say which certificate is missing, which is the thing that decides who gets phoned.

No row actions. Suspend, delete and restore stay on the detail screen. Suspending signs a supplier out of every device immediately — that is a decision to make while looking at who they are, not from a row menu one mis-click away from the row above.

No animation. This is a dense directory worked all day. Nothing here rewards being watched.

No performance columns. Order counts, payouts and ratings have no endpoint yet. A column of dashes beside real figures is how an operator learns to distrust the whole table.
