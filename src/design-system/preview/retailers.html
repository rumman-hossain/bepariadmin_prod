Retailers

Retailers could already sign themselves up. What they could not do is be created, corrected, suspended or reset by the people who answer the phone when a shop cannot log in. These four screens are that half — built on the shape Wholesalers already uses, so an operator who knows one knows both.

1 · The list

One column is new, and it is the one that matters: who created this account. A shop that signed itself up typed its own phone number and its own trade licence; a shop an operator created was transcribed from a phone call. Those two rows deserve different amounts of trust, and until now the screen could not tell them apart.

2 · Onboard retailer — a screen

The prototype does this in a modal. It is the wrong container for the job: this form has five sections, a password, four file uploads and a bank account, and a modal gives it a scrollbar inside a scrollbar, no URL to return to, and no way to recover a half-filled form from a mis-click on the backdrop. It gets a route — /retailers/new — like Add Wholesaler already does.

3 · The document vault

Identity paperwork goes to a bucket with public_access_prevention enforced and no allUsers grant — verified against the project, not assumed. Product photos and shop logos stay in the public bucket, where they belong: they are shown to every browsing buyer, so signing them would cost latency for no confidentiality.

The row states its own security model. A padlock, and the expiry written out in the header rather than discovered when a bookmarked link 403s an hour later. The instinct is to hide this — make it look like an ordinary file list — but an operator who does not know the link dies will paste it into a chat thread and be confused when a colleague cannot open it. The honest version costs one line of type.

"Nobody has checked it yet" rather than "Pending". The badge says Pending because the column does; the line underneath says what that actually means for the person reading it. A pending document is not a slow document — it is one no human has looked at.

4 · Detail, and the two dangerous actions

Suspend and Reset password both get their own route, not a dialog. Both are actions an operator takes while on the phone to somebody, both need a reason typed, and both are the kind of thing that should survive an accidental click elsewhere on the page. A modal is where irreversible actions go to be mis-clicked.

Resetting a password ends the retailer's other sessions, the way changing your own password already does here. A reset that leaves an attacker's session alive has reset nothing.

What these screens deliberately do not have

No credit score field. The column exists, constrained to 0–100 and defaulting to 50 — and nothing in the codebase ever writes it. It appears in three places, all reads. Every retailer has sat at 50 since the table was created. The prototype offers it as an editable number seeded to 600, which the constraint would reject outright. Rendering a figure nobody computes, beside figures that are real, is how an operator learns to distrust the whole screen.

No shop type, decor type, years in business, estimated monthly sale, location ranking or geo-tag. The prototype collects all six. Nothing reads any of them. They would be write-only columns and a longer form for no reader.

No username. Retailers authenticate by phone or email. The prototype has a username field; the schema and the auth path have never had one.

No modal, anywhere. Not for create, not for suspend, not for reset.
