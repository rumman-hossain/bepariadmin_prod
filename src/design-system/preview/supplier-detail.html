What this screen deliberately does not have

No performance tiles, and no apology for them. The screen showed four figures — total orders, total payout, rating, dispatch speed — sourced from functions that returned null unconditionally, and a banner explaining they were not connected. Below it, three more panels sourced from functions returning [], each carrying the same sentence: "This will populate once the endpoint is connected." Five apologies on one screen, occupying two thirds of it. A screen that spends its largest region explaining what it cannot do has been designed around its backlog rather than around its reader.

No products panel. One briefly listed what a supplier sells, on a real endpoint. It was removed as unnecessary — a supplier record is opened to check paperwork and decide, and the catalogue has its own screen with its own supplier filter. It goes back in only if a need for it actually appears.

No status in the page title. "Mayer Doa Store (Active)" makes the name of the business change when it is suspended. The badge carries the state, as it does in the list.

No warning icon on the commission field. It had one. Nothing was wrong. An icon that cries wolf on a routine numeric setting is how people learn to stop reading icons.

No second document vault. The panel is a frame around the shared DocumentVault that the retailer screen also uses. Two vaults would eventually answer "what does a document that is still saving look like" two different ways — the supplier and retailer document rules on the server had already drifted apart that way once.
